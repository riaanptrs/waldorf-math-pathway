# Grade 6–9 Competency Map

This map compares the existing Waldorf Math Pathway with developmental goals described in Jamie York's middle-school curriculum overview. It uses those goals for sequencing only. All digital lessons, examples, prompts, and practice items in this repository are original.

## Design principles

- Understanding comes before a shortcut or formula.
- Every answer should be estimated or predicted, calculated, and checked in its original relationship.
- Major topics return across several years with greater depth instead of appearing once and disappearing.
- Online feedback should reveal one level of support at a time and encourage scratch-paper work, drawing, measuring, and spoken explanation.
- Competency means choosing and checking a method in context, not merely completing one familiar exercise.

## Grade 6

Learners should be able to:

- use all four operations with whole numbers and decimals, including long division;
- move meaningfully among fractions, decimals, division, and familiar percentages;
- estimate before calculating and judge whether an answer is reasonable;
- convert common metric measurements while keeping the unit visible;
- solve accessible percent, unit-cost, discount, interest, and budgeting problems.

Added digital practice:

- `Long Division That Checks Itself`
- `One Number, Three Forms`
- `Measure, Convert, and Judge`
- `Percent Means Out of One Hundred`

## Grade 7

Learners should be able to:

- calculate percent increase or decrease using the original amount as the base;
- reason with ratios, proportions, similar figures, and direct or inverse change;
- distinguish rational approximations from irrational lengths such as square diagonals;
- translate a short situation into an expression or equation and check the solution;
- use the Pythagorean relationship to find a missing side.

Added digital practice:

- `Measure the Change Against the Start`
- `The Diagonal That Does Not End`
- `Turn a Story into an Equation`
- `Find the Missing Side`

## Grade 8

Learners should be able to:

- use conversion factors and dimensional analysis for lengths, rates, areas, and volumes;
- connect direct and inverse proportions to tables, equations, graphs, and units;
- derive and apply area, surface-area, and volume relationships;
- use exponents, signed numbers, fractions, and the distributive property in equations;
- describe and trace a precise algorithm step by step.

Added digital practice:

- `Let the Units Cancel`
- `Rearrange a Trapezoid`
- `Unroll a Cylinder`
- `Trace an Algorithm`

## Grade 9

The source places the bulk of formal algebra after the middle-school introduction. The Grade 9 pathway therefore treats the earlier competencies as foundations and extends them through original Algebra I bridge work.

Learners should be able to:

- connect a linear situation, table, equation, and graph;
- solve equations and systems and interpret the solution in context;
- build a quadratic relationship from a geometric or practical model;
- distinguish additive change from multiplicative growth;
- retain units, reject impossible solutions, and verify results in the original model.

Added digital practice:

- `Connect Table, Rule, and Graph`
- `When Two Plans Cost the Same`
- `Build a Quadratic from Area`
- `Test a Growth Model`

## Recommended practice rhythm

Use one competency lesson as a short diagnostic. If the learner needs support, complete its guided checkpoints and one extra-practice item, then revisit the same competency two or three days later in a new context. Mix one earlier-grade arithmetic item into later work so fractions, decimals, percentages, measurement, and estimation remain active.
